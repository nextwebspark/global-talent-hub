The current Phase 1 approach of asking Claude to generate company names from memory has a fundamental ceiling — Claude reliably knows large conglomerates and manufacturers but does not reliably know specialist B2B distributors, trading houses, and mid-market distribution companies. These are exactly the companies most valuable to a TA professional.
Phase 1 needs to be redesigned around web search as the primary source, not Claude's memory.
New Phase 1 — Web-first seed discovery:
Instead of asking Claude to generate a list, run 4-5 targeted Serper searches designed specifically to find distributor directories, industry lists, and trade association pages. These queries should be constructed to hit high-quality sources:

"{sector} distributor companies {country}"
"{sector} trading companies {country} site:linkedin.com/company"
"top {commercialRole} {sector} {country} {current year}"
"{sector} distribution {country} established companies"
"{country} {sector} {commercialRole} market leaders"

For each search result that is a credible industry source (trade publication, industry directory, LinkedIn, company website) — not a spam aggregator — extract ALL company names mentioned. This is the listicle mining approach we discussed early in this process.
Claude's role shifts to validation and classification only:
Claude does not generate names. Claude only receives names found by web search and answers: is this a real company, is it headquartered in the right geography, and which tier does it belong to (Direct/Adjacent/Excluded). This plays to Claude's strength — validation — rather than its weakness — recall of specialist mid-market companies.
Phase 2 — Claude knowledge supplement:
After web search has produced a seed list, ask Claude: "Are there any well-known {commercialRole} companies in {sector} in {geography} that are not on this list?" This uses Claude to fill gaps rather than as the primary source.
This architecture will find Transmed, BME, Lifco, ASAAT, LG FMCG and Al Seer because they appear in industry articles, directories, and LinkedIn — even if Claude doesn't generate them from memory.
